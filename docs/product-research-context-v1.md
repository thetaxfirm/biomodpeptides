## Primary evidence and its limits

- Lee et al., Cell Metabolism 2015, 21:443–454, DOI 10.1016/j.cmet.2015.02.009. https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1550413115000613 and https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4350682/. Publisher-indexed highlights, summary and results excerpts support a 16-amino-acid mitochondrial-derived peptide, cultured-cell and mouse research, and folate/purine metabolism with AMPK-related signaling. Root independently recovered the indexed primary highlights and summary. Direct full-text retrieval failed (publisher 403; PMC browser challenge); this is not a full-text review. No challenge bypass attempted. Some experiments used engineered expression; the copy does not describe all work as peptide treatment.
- Birk et al., British Journal of Pharmacology 2014, 171:2017–2028, DOI 10.1111/bph.12468. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3976619/. Accessible original methods/results support the four-residue peptide, model lipid membranes, isolated rat-kidney mitochondria, and examinations of cardiolipin interactions, electron transfer, oxygen consumption and ATP synthesis. The paper also uses a modified analogue in some fluorescence measurements; do not imply every assay used unmodified SS-31.

These references are research context, not certificates or evidence that BioMod material was studied. No therapeutic effect, clinical outcome, dosing, safety, superiority, absorption, sequence, salt, purity or supplier endorsement is added. Product lot evidence remains independent. MOTS-c and SS-31 are distinct compounds, not represented as substitutes or a recommended combination.
